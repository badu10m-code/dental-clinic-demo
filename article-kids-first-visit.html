Your child's first dental visit: what we do differently

Dr. Ananya Sharma12 Oct 20265 min read

The first dental visit decides how a child feels about dentists for the next twenty years. That is why our first visits for children are deliberately uneventful.

When to come the first time

By the first birthday, or within six months of the first tooth. Most parents are surprised by how early that is. The point of an early visit is not treatment; it is counting teeth, checking habits, and letting the child learn that this chair is a boring place where nothing hurts.

What the first visit looks like here

  • A ride in the chair, up and down, because the chair is genuinely fun
  • A count of the teeth, out loud, with the child helping
  • A quick look with a mirror, no instruments that whir
  • Ten minutes with the parents on brushing, bottles, and night feeding

Nothing is forced. If a child refuses the mirror today, the mirror waits for next time. A crying first visit buys you nothing and costs you the next two.

The most cavity-causing habit we see in Mumbai is not candy. It is the milk bottle at sleep time. Milk pools around the teeth for hours, and the back teeth of two-year-olds pay for it.

The habits that actually matter

Brush twice daily with a rice-grain amount of fluoride paste until age three, then a pea-sized amount. First dental visit by the first birthday. Sealants on the big back teeth around age six. And an honest conversation about how often sweets happen, because frequency matters more than quantity. Four chocolates spread across the day do more damage than four chocolates in one sitting.
